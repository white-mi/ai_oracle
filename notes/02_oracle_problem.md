## 1. What an oracle is and why blockchains need one

### Takeaway
A blockchain only reaches consensus on data already on its own ledger, so any real-world fact (a price, an election result, whether a company sold bitcoin) must be injected by an "oracle"; that injection step is the trust bottleneck ("oracle problem"). Peer-reviewed surveys and vendor docs agree on this framing, and the 2025 AI-oracle literature stresses that no technique, including AI, removes reliance on unverifiable off-chain inputs.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, vendor] Chainlink defines the oracle problem as "The inability of blockchains to access external data, making them isolated networks, akin to a computer with no Internet connection." It explains that "a blockchain only needs to form consensus on very basic binary questions using data already stored on its own ledger," and that even "What is the market price of Bitcoin?" "can elicit a wide range of different answers that may vary depending on what data source they use," which threatens determinism. Chainlink's answer is "a network of decentralized oracle networks (DONs)" with "decentralization at the node and data source level" — [Chainlink Education Hub: The Oracle Problem](https://chain.link/education-hub/oracle-problem)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Eskandari, Salehi, Gu, Clark, "SoK: Oracles from the Ground Truth to Market Manipulation", arXiv:2106.00667, submitted 1 Jun 2021 (rev. 2 Sep 2021), published at the 3rd ACM Conference on Advances in Financial Technologies (AFT '21), 26–28 Sep 2021, Arlington VA. Abstract: smart contracts "execute in a closed environment... Any interactions with the real world need to be mediated by a bridge service, which is called an oracle"; the SoK "dissect[s] the design alternatives for oracles, showcase[s] attacks, and discuss[es] attack mitigation strategies." — [arXiv:2106.00667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00667)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Pasdar, Dong, Lee, "Blockchain Oracle Design Patterns", arXiv:2106.09349 (cs.CR), submitted 17 Jun 2021; no peer-reviewed venue stated on the page. Classifies oracle designs into two groups by incentive type: voting-based strategies (finalization depends on participant stakes) and reputation-based strategies (reputation plus authenticity proofs); covers stake-based, multisig, Schelling-point and token-based patterns. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.09349); [alphaxiv mirror](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2106.09349.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Caldarelli, "Can Artificial Intelligence solve the blockchain oracle problem? Unpacking the Challenges and Possibilities", arXiv:2507.02125 (University of Turin); published in Frontiers in Blockchain, vol. 8, article 1682623 (type: Review; page shows publication date 3 Dec 2025 and online date 10 Nov 2025, an internal inconsistency on the Frontiers page; note the published title spells "challenges and possibilities" in lower case). Core conclusion: AI (anomaly detection, language-based fact extraction, dynamic reputation modeling, adversarial resistance) improves data quality, source selection and resilience but "cannot eliminate the reliance on unverifiable off-chain inputs"; AI is "a complementary layer of inference and filtering within a broader oracle design, not a substitute for trust assumptions"; the Frontiers abstract adds that the problem "remains epistemological rather than purely technical." — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02125); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/blockchain/articles/10.3389/fbloc.2025.1682623/full)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Cong, Fox, Li, Zhou, "A primer on oracle economics", Journal of Corporate Finance, vol. 94 (2025): "Oracle nodes enable smart contracts to access off-chain and cross-chain data... reliance on external input creates security and reliability risks in information aggregation and transfer." — [IDEAS/RePEc entry](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/corfin/v94y2025ics0929119925000689.html)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Rahman, Al-Chami, Clark, "SoK: Market Microstructure for Decentralized Prediction Markets (DePMs)", arXiv:2510.15612v4 (8 Jul 2026): modern DePMs such as Polymarket "deviate materially from earlier designs like Truthcoin and Augur v1"; the paper models market resolution as one of eight workflow stages and says oracle design for DePMs "remains theoretically underdeveloped" (a stated research gap). — [alphaxiv mirror](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.15612.md)

### Inferences
- For prediction markets the oracle problem is sharper than for price feeds: the question is often subjective/semantic ("did Zelensky wear a suit?"), so the oracle is effectively an adjudicator, not a data pipe. The 2026 Polymarket literature (Section 4) is consistent with this.
- Because every design below either (a) aggregates independent reporters (Chainlink), (b) lets anyone bond a claim and challenge it (UMA OO, Reality.eth), or (c) uses token-weighted/juror voting as a final backstop (UMA DVM, Kleros, Augur), the real differences are in who is trusted at the final step and what it costs to corrupt it.

### Gaps
- I did not find a canonical academic definition that is cited by all authors; the SoK (AFT '21) is the most cited survey I could verify.
- Chainlink's "unverified/ground truth" discussion beyond the education page (e.g., Chainlink 1.0 whitepaper) was not opened.

## 2. Main designs (Chainlink, UMA, Kleros, Reality.eth, Augur, Astraea, SchellingCoin)

### Takeaway
Chainlink aggregates many independent node reports (staking + reputation + BFT-style off-chain reporting) and is the design now used for Polymarket price markets; UMA runs an optimistic oracle (bond, 2-hour challenge window) with a token-weighted Schelling-point vote (DVM) only upon dispute; Kleros, Reality.eth and Augur are the main earlier "human adjudication" designs, each with a different escalation device (juror appeals doubling, bond doubling, REP fork). SchellingCoin (Buterin, 2014) is the common theoretical ancestor of the voting-based designs.

### Cited Findings

#### Chainlink (DONs, Data Feeds, Data Streams)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Chainlink 2.0 whitepaper, "Chainlink 2.0: Next Steps in the Evolution of Decentralized Oracle Networks", authors Breidenbach, Cachin, Chan, Coventry, Ellis, Juels, Koushanfar, Miller, Magauran, Moroz, Nazarov, Topliceanu, Tramèr, Zhang; v1.0, 15 Apr 2021. DONs are committee-run networks providing "networking, storage, and computation"; Off-Chain Reporting (OCR) guarantees the reported median "is equal to or lies between values reported by two honest nodes"; threat model assumes BFT with f < n/3 faulty nodes and explicitly handles "prospective bribery" (conditional advance bribes to randomly selected nodes); staking plus an "Implicit Incentive Framework" counts nodes' future fee opportunities as stake; the paper argues for "super-linear" staking impact so that an adversary's budget must exceed combined node deposits. — [Chainlink 2.0 whitepaper](https://research.chain.link/whitepaper-v2.pdf)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, vendor docs] Data Feeds: "Asset prices, proof of reserves, NAV, and interest rates, aggregated from many sources by decentralized oracle networks and published onchain on dozens of networks." — [Chainlink Data Feeds docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds). (The "Decentralized Data Model" page confirms the design "enables Chainlink to aggregate data from multiple independent node operators," but its body (median, deviation threshold, heartbeat) did not load, see Gaps.) — [Decentralized Data Model](https://docs.chain.link/architecture-overview/architecture-decentralized-model)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, vendor docs] Data Streams: pull-based (not push) design; "Sub-second market data for perpetuals, options, and other latency-sensitive markets"; multiple DON nodes reach consensus and produce signed reports that applications pull via SDK/REST/WebSocket and "verify them onchain in the same transaction that uses them"; components named: DON, Aggregation Network, Verifier contract. — [Chainlink Data Streams docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams)

#### UMA Optimistic Oracle (OOv2 / MOOV2) and DVM
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, UMA docs] Flow: a request (with reward and bond) is created; a third-party proposer posts an answer with a bond; a disputer may challenge during the challenge period ("Each proposal can only be disputed once"); if undisputed the proposal settles and the proposer gets reward plus bond back. Disputes escalate to the Data Verification Mechanism (DVM), a Schelling-point vote: "Stakers commit secret votes during a 24-hour commit period and reveal them in the following 24-hour reveal period"; "Disputes resolve when a minimum 65% majority of staked UMA is cast in favor of a single outcome"; non-voters/minority voters are slashed and the slash is redistributed to the majority; unstaking requires a one-week wait (creating a "cost of corruption" via token depreciation); the page states the OO resolves "99.8%" of requests without DVM escalation. — [UMA docs: How does UMA's oracle work](https://docs.uma.xyz/protocol-overview/how-does-umas-oracle-work)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, UMA docs] DVM 2.0: voters must stake UMA; "48 hour commit-reveal cycle"; two thresholds, GAT ("a constant 5 million UMA" must vote) and SPAT ("65% currently of staked tokens"); slashing "0.1% of your staked amount per vote that is incorrect or missed"; votes failing thresholds "roll" to later rounds and are deleted after rolling a set number of times (4 at launch). — [UMA docs: DVM 2.0](https://docs.uma.xyz/using-uma/voting-walkthrough/dvm-2.0.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, Polymarket help center] Polymarket usage: "Once a market is proposed for resolution it goes into a challenge period of 2 hours"; a dispute requires "a challenge bond of the same amount as the proposer bond (usually $750)"; then "the debate period of 24-48 hours (votes happen every other day and there will always be at least 24 hours for discussion)"; "Uma token holders vote (this process takes approximately 48 hours)"; four vote outcomes: Proposer wins, Disputer wins, Too Early, Unknown/50-50; the page's own arithmetic gives about 74–98 hours from proposal to final resolution. — [Polymarket Help: How are markets disputed](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364551-how-are-markets-disputed)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, UMA blog] MOOV2: UMA's "Managed Proposers" post (12 Aug 2025) introduces a proposer whitelist ("an update to UMA's optimistic oracle") while keeping disputes permissionless; two stated problems: inexperienced proposers submit incorrect answers (needless disputes) and "constant disputes consume time and attention for both integration teams and the UMA community." Launch criteria: "20+ proposals within the past 3 months with over 95% accuracy"; 37 unique addresses met Polymarket's guidelines; whitelisted proposers had "99.7%" cumulative accuracy vs "85.8%" for non-whitelisted and accounted for "96% of all proposals"; dispute rate "~1.3%". — [UMA blog: Managed Proposers](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/managed-proposers)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, UMA docs] Current default whitelist (managed by Risk Labs, applies to ManagedOptimisticOracleV2): eligibility over the preceding 6 months is at least 5 Polymarket or Predict.fun proposals and >95% accuracy; refreshed monthly; front-running leads to immediate removal. (Criteria differ from the Aug 2025 launch blog, so they have changed since.) — [UMA docs: Default proposer whitelist](https://docs.uma.xyz/using-uma/default-proposer-whitelist)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] The Block (Danny Park, 12 Aug 2025): Polymarket's oracle moved from OOV2 to MOOV2, approved via UMA governance proposal UMIP-189 on 6 Aug; initial whitelist of 37 addresses comprising Risk Labs and Polymarket employees plus users with 20+ proposals and 95%+ accuracy; MOOV2 "restricts proposals to a whitelist of experienced proposers to improve due diligence and reduce disputes, particularly in non-contentious markets like sports or crypto prices"; some community members criticized it as moving "from open debates over precedent to a centralized council of proposers." — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/366507/polymarket-uma-oracle-update)

#### Kleros
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, mirror of original] "Kleros (Short Paper v1.0.7)", Lesaege, Ast, George, Sep 2019 (PDF mirrored at gwern.net from kleros.io). Jurors self-select into courts and stake PNK; selection probability scales with stake; votes are committed (hash) then revealed; appeals go to a larger jury, each appeal "doubles the previous juror count plus one," so fees escalate; coherent (majority) jurors keep stake and earn fees, incoherent jurors lose tokens to coherent ones (Schelling-point design). Security discussion: a >50% token acquisition could manipulate verdicts but is claimed to be prohibitively expensive; against bribery/p+epsilon attacks "the possibility of appealing a high number of times is important to prevent an attacker from bribing the jurors"; "Doges on Trial" pilot referenced for p+epsilon testing. Claims are economic, not cryptographic, and depend on an honest majority of tokens. — [Kleros Short Paper v1.0.7 (mirror)](https://www.gwern.net/docs/www/kleros.io/d7e87742c80d3dbcb25e6b275f2105a7c41b05a0.pdf)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, vendor docs] Kleros docs describe it as "a decentralized dispute resolution protocol that uses blockchain and crowdsourced jurors to adjudicate disputes" (the index page lacks mechanism detail). — [Kleros docs](https://docs.kleros.io/)

#### Reality.eth (Realitio)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, project docs] A question is posted with a timeout and an arbitrator contract; anyone may answer with an ETH bond and "Each time they must supply at least double the previous bond"; each new answer resets the timeout; when the timeout lapses the last answer is final; at any point a party can "pay an arbitrator contract to make a final judgement," which freezes the system until the arbitrator rules; wrong answerers' bonds go to correct ones; the arbitrator fee is passed to "untruthful" participants. Security argument: sustained suppression of a true answer requires continually doubling capital. — [Realitio/Reality.eth docs](https://realitio.github.io/docs/html/whitepaper.html). (That Kleros is one available arbitrator was not confirmed on this page.)

#### Augur
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Peterson, Krug, Zoltu, Williams, Alexander, "Augur: a decentralized oracle and prediction market platform", arXiv:1501.01042, submitted 5 Jan 2015 (latest revision v3, 30 Nov 2020). REP holders report outcomes; "Token holders can post progressively-larger Reputation bonds to dispute proposed market outcomes"; at the threshold the system forks, "Reputation splits into multiple versions, one for each possible outcome"; only the version matching reality retains value. — [arXiv:1501.01042](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01042)

#### Astraea
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Adler, Berryhill, Veneris, Poulos, Veira, Kastania, "ASTRAEA: A Decentralized Blockchain Oracle", arXiv:1808.00528, submitted 1 Aug 2018; presented at the IEEE International Conference on Blockchain (Halifax, 2018) per the IEEE Blockchain Technical Brief (which lists only Berryhill and Veneris as authors and a "September 2019" date although the URL says March 2019). Voting game with two roles: low-stake voters assigned propositions at random, and high-stake certifiers who choose propositions; when voters and certifiers agree, correct parties are rewarded, when they disagree certifiers forfeit stake. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.00528); [alphaxiv mirror](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1808.00528.md); [IEEE Blockchain Technical Brief](https://blockchain.ieee.org/technical-briefs/march-2019/astraea-a-decentralized-blockchain-oracle/)

#### SchellingCoin
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Vitalik Buterin, "SchellingCoin: A Minimal-Trust Universal Data Feed", Ethereum Foundation blog, 28 Mar 2014. Participants submit hashed values, then reveal; "Every user who submitted a correctly submitted value between the 25th and 75th percentile gains a reward of N tokens (which we'll call 'schells')." Buterin notes "if any entity controls more than 50% of all votes, they can basically unilaterally set the median to whatever they want," and concludes "a 49% coalition will likely be able to break SchellingCoin, but a 1% coalition will not." — [Ethereum blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/2014/03/28/schellingcoin-a-minimal-trust-universal-data-feed)

#### Polymarket's adoption of Chainlink (Sept 2025)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] The Block (Naga Avan-Nomayo, 12 Sep 2025): Polymarket adopted Chainlink Data Streams (low-latency timestamped price reports) and Chainlink Automation (automatic onchain settlement at set times); "already live on Polygon mainnet for price-based markets, such as crypto pairs"; UMA remains in use for other markets; stated aim includes later testing Chainlink data on more subjective questions and reducing reliance on social voting. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/370444/polymarket-turns-to-chainlink-oracles-for-resolution-of-price-focused-bets)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Decrypt (André Beganski, 12 Sep 2025): Data Streams provides price data on 366 trading pairs (single-source figure); UMA "remains Polymarket's primary oracle system"; the partnership "doesn't replace UMA but complements it for price-based markets." — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/339307/polymarket-taps-chainlink-more-accurate-prediction-market-resolutions)

### Inferences
- Every design pairs a cheap optimistic/aggregation fast path with an expensive escalation path whose credibility is the security assumption. UMA's own numbers (99.8% without DVM; dispute rate 1.3–1.7%) mean the DVM's security matters in the tail of high-value, contentious markets, which is exactly where the empirical incidents occurred (Section 4).
- Chainlink-settled markets are not manipulation-proof either; see Dai, Jia, Yu in Section 5 on Polymarket's five-minute BTC contracts.

### Gaps
- Chainlink Data Feeds details (median aggregation, deviation threshold, heartbeat, minimum responses) not verified: the architecture and OCR doc pages loaded empty. Only the whitepaper statements on OCR median/f<n/3 are verified.
- UMA's original DVM whitepaper (cost-of-corruption vs profit-from-corruption exposition) could not be read (binary/empty). Its claims are [UNVERIFIED] here.
- Kleros live parameters (court sizes, fees) not verified; only the 2019 short paper.
- Whether Reality.eth integrates Kleros as arbitrator was not confirmed from the page I opened.

## 3. The "oracle trilemma"

### Takeaway
The "Oracle Trilemma" (scalability vs decentralization vs truthfulness) was named by Cong, Fox, Li and Zhou in "A primer on oracle economics" (Journal of Corporate Finance, 2025). On the evidence I could open it is a conceptual trade-off framework in a survey/primer, not a proved impossibility theorem. The sentence in your claim appears nearly verbatim in a 2026 arXiv paper that cites Cong et al. and uses the word "formalized."

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY (abstract)] Abstract of Cong, Fox, Li, Zhou (2025): "We describe the general oracle problem, introduce the Oracle Trilemma – highlighting the trade-offs between scalability, decentralization, and truthfulness – and discuss relevant economic issues concerning the role of oracles in applications such as DeFi, supply chain management, gaming, and prediction markets. In particular, we survey off-chain reporting, off-equilibrium alerting, and dynamic incentive design as promising approaches to resolving the trilemma." Journal of Corporate Finance, vol. 94 (2025). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/corfin/v94y2025ics0929119925000689.html)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Kota, "Design and Evaluation of Multi-Agent AI Oracle Systems for Prediction Market Resolution" (Yale; arXiv:2605.30802, 29 May 2026), Sec. 2.2: "No blockchain oracle protocol can simultaneously optimize scalability, decentralization, and truthfulness, a constraint formalized as the Oracle Trilemma [16]," where [16] = Lin William Cong et al., "A primer on oracle economics", J. Corporate Finance (2025). The same paper states "Only approximately 1.5% of proposals are disputed" under UMA's design. — [arXiv:2605.30802 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30802)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY (negative)] Will Cong's Berkeley DeFi lecture slides (Fall 2024, lecture 2) define oracles and the oracle problem ("to centralized or decentralize?") but contain no oracle trilemma slide or formal result. — [Berkeley RDI slides](https://rdi.berkeley.edu/berkeley-defi/assets/material/f24/lec2-will-cong.pdf)

### Inferences
- Who: Cong et al. is the earliest attribution I found, and I did not search for earlier uses of the phrase, so "first" is not established. Where: J. Corporate Finance vol. 94 (2025). Status: the abstract's wording ("introduce... highlighting trade-offs... approaches to resolving the trilemma") fits a heuristic/conceptual taxonomy; an impossibility theorem could not be "resolved" by design approaches. The phrase "formalized" in your claim comes from Kota's paper, which is a citing paper, not the primary source. Recommend wording: "Cong et al. (2025) coined/introduced the Oracle Trilemma as a conceptual trade-off," not "proved."
- The trilemma is analogous in spirit to the blockchain trilemma, which is also a heuristic.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of Cong et al. (paywalled; no SSRN/arXiv copy found), so I cannot rule out a formal model section; the abstract and a citing paper are all I have. The precise definitions of "scalability" and "truthfulness" in the paper are not verified.

## 4. Empirical record: disputes and manipulation on Polymarket/UMA (claims a, c, d, e, f, g)

### Takeaway
The UMA oracle is accurate and rarely disputed in aggregate (about 1–1.7% of proposals disputed; whitelisted proposers about 99.7% accurate by UMA's own count), but the contentious tail has repeatedly produced governance incidents: the March 2025 Ukraine minerals market (a whale with roughly 25% of the vote), the July 2025 Zelensky suit market ($237M), and the May–June 2026 Strategy "sells any bitcoin" market (98.6% No vote, followed by a July 2026 New York lawsuit). A May 2026 WSJ investigation, partly corroborated by Bloomberg, reports heavy vote concentration and conflicts of interest among UMA voters.

### Cited Findings

## 5. What the empirical papers find

### Takeaway
Nechepurenko (Sep 2026) provides the most complete on-chain accounting of Polymarket/UMA adjudication (185,550 adapter-questions; 184,148 requests; 159,447 proposals; 1,604 disputes; 159,133 settlements). Wen et al. (Apr 2026) show LLMs agree 89.58% with UMA's final resolutions on 259 disputed-and-resolved markets, but cannot predict which markets will be disputed. The SoK and Design Patterns papers are 2021 taxonomies; Caldarelli (2025) argues AI cannot remove the trust assumption.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] "Resolution Is Not Settlement, Part I: Oracle Adjudication and Semantic Governance on Polymarket", author Maksym Nechepurenko, arXiv:2609.15368, September 2026 (the arXiv abs page itself did not load through my fetcher; title/author/date from EconPapers, content from the arXiv HTML). Abstract: "Prediction-market resolution is often reduced to a terminal outcome and one timestamp. That representation is inadequate for leveraged event claims because rule versioning, technical request creation, proposal, dispute, reset, Oracle finality, and adapter terminality are distinct states with different observation precision and balance-sheet consequences." Exact counts: 185,550 initialized adapter-question instances; 350,703 decoded adapter logs; 504,332 decoded Oracle lifecycle events, comprising 184,148 request creations, 159,447 proposals, 1,604 disputes and 159,133 settlements; 182,671 questions with at least one request; 1,477 successor generations created through resets; 104,032 questions matched to metadata (56.07% recovery; 81,518 unmatched, zero ambiguous). Median request-to-first-proposal time is 182 s on legacy routes versus 176,388–744,151 s on modern routes; post-reset successor proposals arrive in 300–2,909 s at the median. The versioned rule ledger has 1,570 updates, with exact chain identity timestamping 1,561 of them (including 1,549 creator-authoritative clarifications); of 1,711 exact clarification-generation relations, 83.34% occur after request creation but before first proposal. The mechanism measures request, proposal, dispute, reset, settlement, adapter and clarification timestamps exactly, but not population-wide external-source publication or contractual decidability times. Companion paper: "Resolution Is Not Settlement, Part II: Protocol Finality and Observed Redemption on Polymarket", arXiv:2609.15373 (title only, from a search result; not opened). — [arXiv:2609.15368 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15368); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:arx:papers:2609.15368); [Part II (title only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15373)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Wen, Zhou, Huang, "Can LLMs Help Decentralized Dispute Arbitration? A Case Study of UMA-Resolved Markets on Polymarket", arXiv:2604.15674v1, submitted 17 Apr 2026. Authors: Junhao Wen (Southwest University, Chongqing), Juncen Zhou (University of Sydney), Junjie Huang (corresponding; Southwest University). Abstract: "On Polymarket alone, the trading volume involving disputed events has reached $972,370,804.71" and web-enabled LLMs "achieve 89.58% agreement with UMA's final resolutions and demonstrate strong stability," while "LLMs are unable to reliably predict which events will become disputed in advance." Data: Polymarket API queried three times, latest retrieval UTC 2025-10-27 11:58:47, 140,582 events; 558 user-disputed events narrowed to 259 disputed-and-resolved markets (at least one disputed transition and a terminal resolved state). The 89.58% figure is DeepSeekV3.1: 232 of 259 matches (232/259 = 89.58%, arithmetic checked); Qwen Max 89.19% (internal stability 96.14%; the fetched text spells it "Owen Max," presumably a typo for Qwen Max); gpt-4o-search-preview only 71.43%. The $972M is the volume "involving disputed events" as of the snapshot (a cumulative figure of events that were ever disputed; the paper does not call it money at risk or lost). — [alphaxiv summary of arXiv:2604.15674](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.15674.md); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15674)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] SoK (Eskandari et al.), Pasdar et al. and Caldarelli: details in Section 1. Caldarelli is a position paper/review (no dataset). — see Section 1 links.
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Kota, arXiv:2605.30802 (Yale, 29 May 2026), "Design and Evaluation of Multi-Agent AI Oracle Systems for Prediction Market Resolution": cites "OOTruthBot" reaching "approximately 90% accuracy at roughly $0.005 per resolution" from prior work [10]; I did not verify that prior work, so the OOTruthBot figure is [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv:2605.30802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30802)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Dai, Jia, Yu, "Settlement Manipulation in Prediction Markets", arXiv:2606.31675v1 (fetch reported 24 Aug 2026; the arXiv ID prefix suggests June 2026, so the date is uncertain). Model plus evidence that contracts settling on an asset price that holders can move by trading the underlying transfer wealth from liquidity traders to manipulators. After Polymarket's five-minute Bitcoin contract launched (12 Feb 2026), settlement-time spot order flow spiked (about 50% above pre-launch; 3.9 times in near-the-money cycles), with price reversals within ten seconds after settlement; about 821 manipulators extracted $8.2M across about 1,600 manipulated cycles, "93% of losses fell on retail"; fifteen-minute contracts showed much attenuated manipulation. The paper notes Chainlink aggregates prices across venues by median, yet concentrated pushes on a heavily weighted venue can still move the aggregate. — [arXiv:2606.31675 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31675v1)

### Inferences
- Counting units differ across sources (Oracle "requests," "proposals," "disputes," Polymarket "markets," UMA "assertions," Wen et al. "events"). Do not compare 1,604 (Nechepurenko, whole history), 558 (Wen et al., disputed events to Oct 2025), ">1,150 disputed markets so far in 2026" (Finance Magnates) and "nearly 2,000 disputed and resolved in the past year" (Bloomberg via Outlook Money) as if they were the same quantity. Taken together they are at least consistent with a sharp rise in disputes in 2026 (558 by Oct 2025 versus more than 1,150 in 2026 alone), with the caveat on units.
- Wen et al.'s negative result (LLMs cannot predict disputes from rule text) plus the reported dispute origin ("external real-world uncertainty, evolving evidence, reporting inconsistencies, and delays in authoritative confirmation") supports the view that disputes are driven by the world, not by ambiguous wording alone. Nechepurenko's finding that 83.34% of clarifications arrive after request creation but before first proposal quantifies the "semantic governance" issue underlying the Strategy case (clarification timing).
- 89.58% agreement measures agreement with UMA's token-vote outcome, not with ground truth; given Section 4's conflict-of-interest concerns, agreement with UMA is a proxy for "predicting the Schelling point," not accuracy.

## 6. Formal and game-theoretic results (optimistic oracles, Schelling voting, bribery, peer prediction)

### Takeaway
The verified formal literature is thin and mostly pre-2021: SchellingCoin (49% coalition breaks it), the p+epsilon attack (a conditional bribe costs nothing in equilibrium), Chainlink 2.0 (BFT plus super-linear staking against prospective bribery), Astraea (honest Nash equilibrium for adversary fraction below 0.5), Infochain (peer-consistency mechanism truthful even with outside incentives, with an explicit scaling-constant bound), Kleros (appeals as bribery defence), Reality.eth (bond doubling). I found no peer-reviewed formal security analysis of UMA's optimistic oracle or of its token-weighted DVM (cost of corruption vs profit from corruption, bond sizing) in the sources I could open.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] SchellingCoin (Buterin, 28 Mar 2014): 25th–75th percentile reward rule; control of more than 50% of votes lets an entity set the median; a 1% coalition offering a tiny side-payment faces a "massive coordination problem," while "a 49% coalition will likely be able to break SchellingCoin." — [Ethereum blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/2014/03/28/schellingcoin-a-minimal-trust-universal-data-feed)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] p+epsilon attack (Buterin, "The P + Epsilon Attack", 28 Jan 2015): in a Schelling game where majority voters earn P, the attacker commits to pay X = P + epsilon to voters for the attacker's preferred outcome 1 if the majority votes against it, and X = 0 if the majority votes for it. Then "it's a dominant strategy for anyone to vote 1 no matter what you think the majority will do." So everyone votes 1, the attacker wins and never pays; "the attacker takes on the failure risk themselves." This breaks pure Schelling-point incentive logic regardless of the share of attacker-controlled tokens, so the defences are non-pure-Schelling devices: appeals/forks/slashing, or intersubjective checks. — [Ethereum blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/2015/01/28/p-epsilon-attack)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Chainlink 2.0 (Apr 2021): cost-of-corruption vs profit-from-corruption framing; "an adversary must have a budget B greater than the combined deposits of all oracle nodes"; "super-linear" staking impact; BFT assumption f < n/3; prospective bribery explicitly in scope; stake includes future fee opportunities (Implicit Incentive Framework). — [Chainlink 2.0 whitepaper](https://research.chain.link/whitepaper-v2.pdf)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Astraea (Adler et al., 2018): two-tier voter/certifier game; the probability an adversary forces a wrong outcome depends on adversarial vote fraction and the paper gives the expression "1 − q + n·q/(|P|·D_v)," which must be less than 0.5 for secure parameters; an honest-play Nash equilibrium exists when honest voters keep a sufficient accuracy plurality; participation profitability requires bounties satisfying B_j ≤ (1−q_i)·D_v/q_i. (Symbol definitions were not verified; use qualitatively.) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.00528); [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1808.00528.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Infochain (Goel, van Schreven, Filos-Ratsikas, Faltings), IJCAI-2020 (Special Track on AI in FinTech, pp. 4604–4610, doi:10.24963/ijcai.2020/635); arXiv:1908.10258v2 (26 Jul 2020). Shows "a peer-consistency incentive mechanism can be used to acquire truthful information from an untrusted and self-interested crowd, even when the crowd has outside incentives to provide wrong information." Theorem 1: the Peer Truth Serum for Crowdsourcing (PTSC) has a strict truthful equilibrium even with outside incentives when the scaling constant satisfies alpha > c·(1 + (n−1)·gamma)/(n·beta); Theorem 2: expected per-agent payment alpha suffices; Theorem 3: expected relative saving versus naive refunding is at least P(1)(2 − P(1)) − alpha/c. Also an Ethereum implementation with gas-cost optimizations, commit-reveal and random peer selection. (Variable meanings not verified; the page is a summary of the arXiv text.) — [IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/635); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10258); [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1908.10258.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Kleros (2019): appeals with doubling juror counts, fees escalating, plus argument that PNK price impact makes bribing the final court uneconomical; relies on an honest token majority. — [Kleros short paper mirror](https://www.gwern.net/docs/www/kleros.io/d7e87742c80d3dbcb25e6b275f2105a7c41b05a0.pdf)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Reality.eth: doubling bond escalation makes attacking a true answer cost exponentially more capital; arbitration fee pushed to the untruthful side. Augur: progressively larger REP bonds and a fork where the false-outcome REP becomes worthless. — [Realitio docs](https://realitio.github.io/docs/html/whitepaper.html); [Augur whitepaper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01042)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, UMA docs] UMA's security rationale: slashing (0.1% of stake per missed/incorrect vote), staking and a one-week unstaking lockup so a successful attack depreciates the attacker's holdings ("cost of corruption"); the optimistic layer makes the DVM a rarely invoked backstop (99.8% of requests never reach it). The docs do not quantify a bribery cost. — [UMA docs](https://docs.uma.xyz/protocol-overview/how-does-umas-oracle-work); [DVM 2.0](https://docs.uma.xyz/using-uma/voting-walkthrough/dvm-2.0.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, UMA blog] UMA/Polymarket/EigenLayer research agenda explicitly names p+epsilon-style bribery as an open threat for Schelling-point oracles and proposes "intersubjective" security (identifying bribery itself as an intersubjective truth). No results published as of my search. — [UMA blog](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/uma-polymarket-and-eigenlayer-research-a-next-gen-prediction-market-oracle)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Dai, Jia, Yu (2026): a formal model of settlement manipulation for price-settled prediction contracts, supported by Polymarket five-minute BTC data (Section 5). — [arXiv:2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31675v1)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] The SoK on DePMs says oracle design for DePMs "remains theoretically underdeveloped." — [alphaxiv mirror](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.15612.md)

### Inferences
- Cost of corruption vs profit from corruption: for token-weighted voting the relevant comparison is the attacker's cost to acquire or rent a majority (or a pivotal minority under low turnout) versus the value of the disputed market. The Ukraine case (about 5M UMA, about 25% of votes, with about 20M UMA staked per one secondary source) shows that when turnout is low and market value is in the millions, the pivotal stake can be a modest fraction of supply. This is my reading of the incident, not a result from a paper.
- p+epsilon is a dominant-strategy bribery argument that does not depend on controlling a large share of the stake, whereas SchellingCoin's basic attack needs a majority. UMA's slashing/lockup raises cost for a bribed majority (collateral loss on token price) but does not by itself remove the p+epsilon incentive, which is why UMA/EigenLayer list it as a research item. This is an inference from the sources above.
- Peer-prediction/peer-consistency designs (Infochain/PTSC) are the one line of formal work in my sources that explicitly handles outside incentives (bribes, positions in the market) with a quantitative truthfulness bound. They require an exogenous scale constant and many reporters, and have not, as far as I found, been adopted by UMA or Polymarket.
- WSJ's finding that about 1 in 5 disputes had a voter with a position is a natural application of the "outside incentives" model in Infochain, which could be cited as the theoretical frame for a vote-weighting or exclusion design.

### Gaps
- No formal paper found that analyses UMA's OO bond sizing (e.g., minimum bond relative to market value, grief-resistance) or the token-weighted DVM against p+epsilon quantitatively. The UMA DVM whitepaper (which uses a cost-of-corruption/profit-from-corruption argument) could not be opened, so that framing is [UNVERIFIED] for UMA, though verified for Chainlink 2.0.
- Variable definitions in the Astraea and Infochain theorem statements were not verified from the full text; the Peer Truth Serum original (Faltings et al.) and Goel et al.'s separate "outcome-dependent lying incentives" work were not opened (the Infochain paper is the one verified).
- Truthcoin/Sztorc, and the Thomas Schelling "focal point" origin, were not opened.

# Reference-list and citation-accuracy audit: Kota, "Design and Evaluation of Multi-Agent AI Oracle Systems for Prediction Market Resolution" (arXiv:2605.30802v1, 29 May 2026)

Audit date: 5 Oct 2026. Scope: the 35 numbered references printed on PDF pp. 28-30, every in-text attribution of a number or specific claim to a reference, uncited statements, and unused .bib entries. The arXiv listing for the paper itself exists (Tarun Kota; cs.MA; 34 pages, 11 figures; v1 29 May 2026) [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30802).

## Key question 1: Does each of the 35 bibliography entries exist, and is its metadata (authors, title, year, venue, id, URL) correct?

### Takeaway
All 35 printed references point to something that exists except one ([33], not matched to any record I could open). Only 15 entries are correct as printed; 12 have substantive metadata errors (wrong first names, wrong titles, "Anonymous" authors, wrong pages), 2 have minor discrepancies, 2 are correct but print without a venue, 3 could not be fully verified, and 1 could not be matched. The two specific suspicions are confirmed: [12] is by Lukas Nel (not Ethan), and arXiv:2506.11887 is Fanconi and van der Schaar's "Cascaded Language Models for Cost-effective Human-AI Decision-Making", not an anonymous "Three-tier LLM-human cascaded architectures" paper.

### Cited Findings

#### Method, legend and access limits
- Each row was checked on a primary page (arXiv abs/PDF, publisher or proceedings page, blog URL) with the WebFetch tool, which returns page text passed through a summarising model; quotes are as returned. Where no primary page could be opened, the registry record (OpenAlex or Crossref) was used and the row says so: [19] pages, [20] pages, [22] all fields, [24] journal fields. Decisive findings were re-read with a second prompt or a second source (author names of [9], [11], [12], [24]; the UMA numbers; the Du et al. table; the Cemri percentages; the KalshiBench setup; the Kalshi help page).
- Status legend: **Correct as printed** = every printed field matched; **Metadata correct; printed entry loses venue** = the .bib uses @article with a booktitle field so the PDF prints "In: 119 (2020)" or "In: (2025)"; **Correct on fields checked** = no discrepancy found but some field could not be confirmed on a publisher page; **METADATA ERROR** = at least one printed field contradicts the primary page; **Minor discrepancy** = title wording or byline only; **NOT FOUND** = no matching record found.
- Pages that could not be read: ACM Digital Library (HTTP 403), PubMed and Europe PMC and the Semantic Scholar API (HTTP 429, proxy said not to retry), dblp (robots.txt), IEEE Xplore page for Chow 1970 (empty), scinapse (reCAPTCHA), the PMLR PDF for Du et al. (404; the PMLR HTML page worked), arXiv search (robots.txt). Plain curl and the arXiv export API are blocked by the egress proxy (HTTP 403). WebSearch quota (200 calls) ran out near the end; before that, a search for the exact title of [33] found nothing, and the cited title of [35] was never searched.

#### The two UMA OOTruthBot numbers (about 90% and about 78%)
- Intro (p2) says "approximately 90% accuracy at roughly $0.005 per resolution"; Related Work (p22) says "approximately 78% overall accuracy (up to 95% on objective events with clear data) at roughly $0.005"; both cite [10], the 30 Jul 2025 post "Inside UMA's Optimistic Truth Bot", which has no accuracy figure and no cost figure ([UMA post](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/inside-umas-optimistic-truth-bot)).
- The 3 Jun 2025 post "UMA's AI Experiment: Can AI Agents Enhance the Optimistic Oracle?" has both numbers: TL;DR "upwards of 90% accuracy on real prediction markets" and body "The current running real-time accuracy of the Optimistic Truth Bot is ~78% across all market types"; also 99.3% on sports and asset pricing, 72% on mention markets ignoring P4, "over 3000 markets", and "80% of current disputes" caused by proposals made too early ([UMA 3 Jun](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/experiment-can-ai-agents-enhance-uma-oracle)).
- The 30 Jun 2025 post "AI Is Helping Us Find the Truth. Here's How." says "~95% accuracy when excluding early or ambiguous cases" and "overall accuracy across all markets drops to ~78%" ([UMA 30 Jun](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/ai-is-helping-us-find-the-truth)).
- A cost of $0.005 per resolution does not appear in any of the three posts (none of them contains a per-resolution cost figure). So the 90% and 78% are two different figures from the sibling posts (TL;DR headline versus running accuracy), and the paper uses one in the Introduction and the other in Related Work for the same system under the same reference.

### Inferences
- In several cases the number exists in a different source than the one cited: Chainalysis [15] for C6, the UMA 3 Jun and 30 Jun posts for C8 and C40, a Sacra estimate for C2. For C21-C23 I found no source.
- The in-text paraphrases of the academic papers are mostly faithful on substance (Kim, SycEval, Estornell and Liu, Wynn et al., Mozannar and Sontag, Bansal et al., Jung et al., Omar et al. headline numbers). The errors cluster in specific quantitative or comparative details (Du, Cemri, FEVER, KalshiBench setup).

### Gaps
- Claim-level checks of books and paywalled articles were not possible: Laughlin (C51), the full text of Cong et al. (C15), Arrow et al. text (C1), the journal version of ICE (C33; I read the medRxiv preprint, whose numbers could differ).

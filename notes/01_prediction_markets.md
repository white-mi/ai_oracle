## 1. Fundamentals, market mechanisms, and foundational academic references

### Takeaway
A prediction-market contract is a binary claim that pays $1 if an event happens and $0 otherwise, so its price in dollars is read as the market's probability; both Kalshi (order book, $1 notional per contract) and Polymarket (hybrid CLOB, tokens fully backed by $1 collateral) implement exactly this. The foundational literature (Hayek 1945 -> Wolfers-Zitzewitz 2004 -> Arrow et al. 2008) argues markets aggregate dispersed information; Manski (2006) and Wolfers-Zitzewitz (2006) are the standard cautions that price = probability only under conditions. Hanson's LMSR is the canonical automated market maker with worst-case loss b*ln(N).

### Cited Findings
**Core contract mechanics**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Kalshi settlement docs: "Yes contract holders receive $1 per contract" if the yes outcome occurs (reverse for no); "Only net positions are settled (after netting)"; positions are paid out automatically — [Kalshi docs: Market Settlement](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/market_settlement.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Live Kalshi market objects carry `notional_value_dollars: "1.0000"`, `market_type: "binary"`, prices as dollar strings (e.g. `last_price_dollars: "0.0083"`), and `price_level_structure: "center_deci_edge_centi_cent"` with price ranges 0.0000-0.0100 step 0.0001, 0.0100-0.9900 step 0.0010, 0.9900-1.0000 step 0.0001 — [live API response](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?status=settled&limit=2)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Polymarket docs: outcome tokens are ERC-1155 on Polygon built on the Gnosis Conditional Token Framework; "Every Yes/No pair in existence is backed by exactly $1 of pUSD collateral locked in the CTF contract"; operations are split ($100 -> 100 Yes + 100 No), merge (reverse) and redeem (winner = $1, loser = $0) — [Polymarket docs: Positions & Tokens](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/positions-tokens.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Polymarket docs now name the collateral token pUSD ("the collateral token used for all trading on Polymarket"); older writing says USDC/USDC.e — [Polymarket docs index](https://docs.polymarket.com/llms.txt), [Resolution page](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution.md). I did not find a page explaining when/why the migration to pUSD happened.
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Generic definition: binary contracts priced between $0.01 and $1 "represent the odds of an event occurring"; winners pay $1; Iowa Electronic Markets launched 1988; PredictIt got a CFTC no-action letter in 2014 — [Wikipedia: Prediction market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_market)

**Mechanisms: order book / CLOB, LMSR, AMM**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Kalshi exposes a per-market order book endpoint (`GET /markets/{ticker}/orderbook`, "showing bid orders"), i.e. a central limit order book exchange — [Kalshi docs index](https://docs.kalshi.com/llms.txt), [Quick start: market data](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/quick_start_market_data.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Polymarket CLOB is hybrid: orders are matched off-chain, trades "settle on-chain asynchronously"; orders are EIP-712 signed — [Polymarket docs: CLOB intro](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/CLOB/introduction)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] LMSR: for the logarithmic scoring rule s_i(r) = b*log(r_i), the equivalent market-maker cost function is C(q) = b*log(sum_j exp(q_j/b)); prices are partial derivatives p_i = dC/dq_i; with uniform initial distribution "the maximum amount to lose is bounded by b log N" (N = number of outcomes) — Chen & Pennock, "A Utility Framework for Bounded-Loss Market Makers", UAI 2007 — [arXiv:1206.5252](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5252) (full text: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5252))
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Hanson's early LMSR working paper (GMU, Jan 2002) gives the exponential market-maker price m_i(x) = exp((-a_i - x_i)/b) / sum_j exp((-a_j - x_j)/b), and the max expected payment as entropy -b*sum pi_i log(pi_i); the working-paper text I read does NOT state the "b ln n" loss bound explicitly (use Chen-Pennock for that) — [Hanson, mktscore.pdf](https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/mktscore.pdf)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Gnosis CTF docs name "Omen and Polymarket" as implementations of Conditional Tokens and state the framework is not tied to any specific oracle — [Gnosis CTF docs](https://docs.gnosis.io/conditionaltokens/)

### Inferences
- Kalshi's price grid (0.1c ticks at the extremes, 0.1c-1c in the middle) means quoted prices are directly interpretable as probabilities to about 0.1 pp in the bulk, but the Manski/Wolfers-Zitzewitz caveats still apply (heterogeneous beliefs, risk attitudes, fees).
- Neither Kalshi nor Polymarket uses LMSR as its main mechanism (both are order-book venues), so LMSR is historically/theoretically important rather than the live design of the two dominant platforms. I did not verify any platform that still runs LMSR in 2026.

## 2. The two dominant platforms: regulatory history, structure, and scale

### Takeaway
Kalshi is a CFTC-designated contract market (DCM; Nov 2020) whose September 2024 district-court win (opinion dated 12 Sept 2024, Judge Cobb) let it list election contracts and whose appeal was dropped by the CFTC on 5 May 2025; Polymarket is an on-chain venue (Polygon, Conditional Tokens, UMA oracle) that settled with the CFTC in 2022, bought the DCM QCEX in July 2025 (reported $112M), and got an amended CFTC order on 24 Nov 2025 allowing FCM-intermediated US trading. By 2026 Kalshi is the larger venue by most trackers (78% of prediction-market volume in a Sept-Oct 2026 window per DeFiRate).

## 3. Kalshi: the market record, how outcomes are determined, and the public API

### Takeaway
Kalshi is a three-level hierarchy (series -> event -> market) with market objects that carry rules (`rules_primary`, `rules_secondary`), three time concepts (`close_time`, `expected_expiration_time`/`latest_expiration_time`, `settlement_ts`), and a `result` of yes/no/scalar. Kalshi itself (the exchange) determines the outcome from a named source agency; there is a post-determination "settlement timer" during which a result can be disputed/amended. Market data is public and needs no authentication; settled markets are in two partitions (live `/markets` and `/historical/markets`, split at a moving cutoff, currently 2026-08-05).

### Cited Findings
**Record structure (verified against the live API, 2026-10-04)**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Hierarchy: a Series is "a template for recurring events that follow the same format" (ticker, frequency, title, category, tags, `settlement_sources` = "official sources used for the determination of markets within the series", `contract_url` = link to the original filing of the underlying contract, `contract_terms_url`, `fee_type` quadratic/flat, `fee_multiplier`); Events group "multiple outcome markets" (`event_ticker`, `series_ticker`, `title`, `sub_title`, `mutually_exclusive`, `collateral_return_type` MECNET/DIRECNET, `strike_date`/`strike_period`, `settlement_sources`); Markets are the binary tradables — [Series endpoint](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-series.md), [Events endpoint](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/events/get-events), [Docs index](https://docs.kalshi.com/llms.txt)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Market object fields (docs): `ticker`, `event_ticker`, `market_type` ("binary" or "scalar"), `yes_sub_title`, `no_sub_title`, `created_time`, `open_time`, `close_time`, `expected_expiration_time`, `latest_expiration_time`, `settlement_timer_seconds`, `status`, bid/ask in `*_dollars` fixed-point strings, `last_price_dollars`, `volume_fp`, `volume_24h_fp`, `open_interest_fp`, `notional_value_dollars`, `result` ("yes","no","scalar", or empty), `settlement_value_dollars`, `settlement_ts`, `expiration_value` ("value evaluated for settlement"), `occurrence_datetime`, `can_close_early`, `early_close_condition`, `rules_primary` ("core market term summary"), `rules_secondary`, `strike_type`/`floor_strike`/`cap_strike`/`custom_strike`, `price_level_structure`, `price_ranges`, `mve_collection_ticker`, `mve_selected_legs`, `is_provisional`, `exchange_index` — [Get Markets docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-markets)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Live example (ordinary prop market), ticker `KXNFLREC-26OCT04KCLV-LVTTUCKER1-1` (series `KXNFLREC`, event `KXNFLREC-26OCT04KCLV`): `rules_primary` = "If Tre Tucker records 1+ receptions in the Kansas City vs Las Vegas Pro Football game originally scheduled for Oct 4, 2026, then the market resolves to Yes."; `rules_secondary` says the market settles at fair price if the player stays inactive; `close_time` 2026-10-04T22:50:48Z; `expected_expiration_time` 2026-10-05T02:25:00Z; `expiration_time` 2026-10-06T20:25:00Z; `settlement_ts` 2026-10-04T22:52:47Z; `status` finalized; `result` yes; `can_close_early` true, `early_close_condition` "This market will close and expire early if the event occurs."; `expiration_value` "receptions" — [live API, mve_filter=exclude](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?status=settled&limit=2&mve_filter=exclude)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Live example from the historical partition: `KXDIMAYORTOTAL-26AUG04LLAFOR-7`, title "Will over 6.5 goals be scored?", `rules_primary` "If over 6.5 goals are scored in the Llaneros vs Fortaleza professional Colombian Liga DIMAYOR soccer game originally scheduled for Aug 4, 2026 after 90 minutes plus stoppage time (does not include extra time or penalties), then the market resolves to Yes."; `close_time` 2026-08-04T23:32:57Z; `expiration_time` 2026-08-07T03:10:00Z; `settlement_ts` 2026-08-04T23:34:30Z; `result` no; `status` finalized — [live API /historical/markets](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/historical/markets?limit=1&mve_filter=exclude)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Close vs expiration vs settlement, per docs: `close_time` = "When trading stops. May be moved earlier if `can_close_early` is true"; `expected_expiration_time` = "when the outcome is likely resolved"; `latest_expiration_time` = "furthest possible expiration date"; settlement = after the result is set and the timer runs, "positions are paid out". In the live JSON, `expiration_time` is present and equals `latest_expiration_time` in all samples I saw, but the docs' field table I read does not list `expiration_time` — [Market lifecycle docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/market_lifecycle)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Lifecycle statuses: `initialized`, `active`, `inactive` ("temporarily deactivated by the exchange"), `closed` ("past close_time ... awaiting determination"), `determined` (outcome known, settlement timer running), `disputed` ("result has been challenged. May be re-determined"), `amended` ("re-determined after a dispute. Settlement timer restarts"), `finalized` ("positions have been paid out. Terminal state"). Query filter values differ from object statuses: the list endpoint filters by `unopened|open|paused|closed|settled`, while settled objects show `status: "finalized"` — [Market lifecycle](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/market_lifecycle), [Get Markets](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-markets), live JSON above
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] `settlement_timer_seconds` was 5 in the multivariate (combo) markets I sampled, and `settlement_ts` came ~2 min after `close_time` in the ordinary sports markets sampled (the non-MVE extraction did not show the timer field) — [live API, default listing](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?status=settled&limit=2), [live API, mve_filter=exclude](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?status=settled&limit=2&mve_filter=exclude)

**Who decides and how**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Kalshi help center: "Once a contract has expired, Kalshi determines the market's outcome based on these rules"; outcomes are verified against "the source agency we use to verify the outcome of the market, as defined in the market contract"; "Determination can take anywhere from one hour to more than twelve hours to complete aftermarket closure," depending on when the source agency delivers data. The page does not discuss disputes or early close — [Kalshi Help: Market outcomes](https://help.kalshi.com/markets/markets-101/market-outcomes). (Tension with live data: sports prop markets finalized ~2 minutes after close; the help page is a generic upper-bound statement.)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Multi-outcome events are decomposed into binary markets under one event; `mutually_exclusive` / `collateral_return_type: MECNET` marks events where exactly one market can win (DIRECNET = directional) — [Events endpoint](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/events/get-events)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Multivariate ("combo/parlay") markets are a separate class (`mve_collection_ticker`, `mve_selected_legs`, e.g. `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY-...`); they have empty `rules_primary`, a boilerplate `rules_secondary`, and legs listed in `custom_strike` — [live API default listing](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?status=settled&limit=2). DeFiRate notes combos were 22% of "dollars paid" in a recent window, each leg counted as a $1 contract — [DeFiRate Kalshi](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/volume/kalshi/)

### Inferences
- For an oracle/LLM-resolution dataset, the clean unit is a non-MVE binary market whose `rules_primary` is the question, `result` the label, `close_time`/`settlement_ts` the timeline; `custom_strike` and `settlement_sources` (series level) give resolution provenance. Resolution is rule-text-driven and often automatic (data feed + source agency), which suits automated oracle testing.
- `result` can also be "scalar" and `settlement_value_dollars` can be fractional ("fair price" settlement for inactive players), so a naive yes/no filter will drop or mislabel some markets.
- Because the cutoff moves, a snapshot taken before/after 2026-08-05 (or any later cutoff update) changes which endpoint holds a given market; fetch the cutoff first and dedupe by `ticker`.

## 4. Polymarket: architecture, resolution via UMA, multi-outcome decomposition

### Takeaway
Polymarket markets are binary Yes/No ERC-1155 token pairs (Gnosis CTF on Polygon) traded on a hybrid off-chain CLOB; multi-outcome events are bundles of binary markets linked by the "neg risk" adapter. Resolution is optimistic: anyone proposes an outcome with a bond (typically $750 pUSD), a 2-hour challenge window follows, a first dispute resets the proposal round, a second dispute escalates to UMA's token-holder DVM vote (~48 h; total 4-6 days). A real governance-attack episode (Ukraine minerals market, March 2025, ~$7M) shows the failure mode.

### Cited Findings
**Market/event record**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Definitions: a market is "a single binary question with Yes/No outcomes" (Condition ID, Question ID, two ERC-1155 token IDs); an event "groups one or more related markets"; multi-outcome events can be "mutually exclusive" so "exactly one market in the group resolves Yes, while every other market resolves No" — [Polymarket docs: Markets & Events](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/markets-events.md)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Live Gamma API record (no auth): market fields `id`, `question`, `conditionId`, `slug`, `resolutionSource`, `description`, `outcomes`, `outcomePrices`, `volume`, `closed`, `closedTime`, `umaResolutionStatus`, `negRisk`, `endDate`, `automaticallyResolved`, `bestBid`, `bestAsk`. Example: "Will Donald Trump win the 2024 US Presidential Election?" volume $1,531,479,284.50, `umaResolutionStatus: "resolved"`, `negRisk: true`, `closedTime` 2024-11-06 15:17:41+00, `outcomePrices` ["1","0"] — [Gamma /markets](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/markets?closed=true&limit=1&order=volumeNum&ascending=false). Event record: "World Cup Winner" (`negRisk: true`, 26 markets, volume ~$4.33B), each nested market has own `conditionId`, `clobTokenIds`, `groupItemTitle` (e.g. "Spain"), `umaResolutionStatus` — [Gamma /events](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?limit=1&closed=true&order=volume&ascending=false). Note `resolutionSource` was an empty string in all samples I saw; the resolution rules live in `description`.
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Neg-risk: "A No share in any market can be converted into 1 Yes share in every other market" via the Neg Risk Adapter; "augmented" neg-risk adds placeholder and explicit "Other" outcomes, with the trading rule to trade only named outcomes; if the true winner is not named the event resolves to "Other" — [Polymarket docs: neg risk](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/neg-risk/overview)

**UMA optimistic-oracle resolution**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Flow: "Anyone can propose a resolution" by selecting the winning outcome and posting a bond "(typically $750 pUSD)"; "2-hour challenge period where anyone can dispute"; no dispute -> resolves (~2 h total); one dispute -> a new proposal round (disputer posts a matching counter-bond); a second dispute -> escalation to UMA DVM token-holder vote; "24-48 hour debate period" in UMA's Discord, then ~48 h voting; total for a disputed resolution 4-6 days; unclear outcomes resolve 50/50 (each token redeems for $0.50); winners redeem $1 through the CTF collateral adapter; UmaCtfAdapter v3.0 at 0x157Ce2d672854c848c9b79C49a8Cc6cc89176a49 on Polygon (v1.0 and v2.0 also exist) — [Polymarket docs: Resolution](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution.md); older prose version of the same ($750 bond, 2-hour challenge period) in [Polymarket Help Center](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364518-how-are-prediction-markets-resolved)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] UMA protocol side: requests specify resolution criteria, reward, bond and minimum challenge period; undisputed proposals settle and the proposer gets reward plus bond back; disputes go to the DVM; stakers "commit secret votes during a 24-hour commit period and reveal them in the following 24-hour reveal period" (48 h); "Disputes resolve when a minimum 65% majority of staked UMA is cast in favor of a single outcome"; minority voters are slashed; one-week unstaking period — [UMA docs](https://docs.uma.xyz/protocol-overview/how-does-umas-oracle-work)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Governance attack: March 24-25, 2025, a ~$7M market ("Ukraine agrees to Trump mineral deal before April?") swung from 9% to 100% Yes and resolved Yes after a large UMA holder allegedly voted decisively; Polymarket declined refunds ("not a market failure") and said it was in "war rooms" with UMA to prevent recurrence; the article says it was the second controversial resolution after a prior overturned UMA decision (Barron Trump/DJT token market) — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Sacra's description: "UMA's decentralized oracle writes resolution results on-chain, meaning Polymarket never touches user funds or settlement" — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/polymarket/)

### Inferences
- Kalshi vs Polymarket resolution contrast: Kalshi = centralized exchange determination against a named source agency, fast (minutes to hours), with an exchange-controlled dispute/amend path; Polymarket (global) = permissionless proposal + economic dispute, resolved by rule text in the market `description`, with human token-holder arbitration only on a second dispute (days). The "resolution criteria" artifact is the market description/rules text in both.
- Early close: Kalshi has an explicit `can_close_early` / `early_close_condition` field (closes on event occurrence); on Polymarket "early resolution" is simply an early proposal (a premature wrong proposal forfeits the bond, per the Help Center sentence "If you propose a market too early ... you will lose all of your $750 bond"). I did not find a separate Polymarket "early close" mechanism.

### Gaps
- UMA's "Managed Optimistic Oracle V2" / proposer whitelisting (reported after the March 2025 incident) was not found in the docs I read, and the docs I read say "anyone can propose"; whether the whitelist is live in Oct 2026 is UNVERIFIED.
- Which oracle/rules Polymarket US (the DCM) uses for resolution was not found.
- Polymarket's pre-CLOB AMM era and the date/cause of the pUSD migration not verified.

## 6. Empirical accuracy and calibration of prediction-market prices

### Takeaway
Markets beat polls on average in the best-studied case (Iowa Electronic Markets vs 964 polls, 1988-2004: market closer 74% of the time) and show a robust favorite-longshot bias, which grows with time-to-expiry (Page & Clemen 2013) and is visible on Kalshi (contracts at 1-10 cents lost 60%+; Bürgi-Deng-Whelan 2026). Polymarket self-reports 90.1% accuracy one month before resolution, but that figure is vulnerable to market-mix effects (77.8% of markets resolve No). Recent large-sample work finds calibration depends on category, time and trade size.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Berg, Nelson & Rietz, "Prediction market accuracy in the long run", International Journal of Forecasting 24(2):285-300 (2008) (IDEAS lists pp. 285-300; the arXiv:2605.30802 reference list gives 283-298): Iowa Electronic Markets vote-share predictions vs 964 national polls over five US presidential elections (1988-2004); the market was "closer to the eventual outcome 74% of the time" and "significantly outperforms the polls in every election when forecasting more than 100 days in advance" — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v24y2008i2p285-300.html)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Page & Clemen, "Do Prediction Markets Produce Well-Calibrated Probability Forecasts?", Economic Journal 123(568):491-513, May 2013. Model predicts time to expiration should reduce price accuracy, with likely events underpriced and unlikely events overpriced; transaction data confirm: reasonable accuracy for near-term events, significant bias for distant ones; only traders with low discount rates can profit — [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/765630), [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v123y2013i568p491-513.html)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (UCD), "Makers and Takers: The Economics of the Kalshi Prediction Market" (version dated January 2026): 313,972 price observations on 46,282 contracts across 12,403 events, 2021 - April 2025; "a clear favorite-longshot bias: low-price contracts win far less often than required to break even after fees, while high-price contracts win more often and yield small positive returns"; contracts priced 1-10 cents average -60%+; overall average return -20%; makers -9.64%, takers -31.46%; pre-2025 taker fee $0.07*P*(1-P) per contract, makers no fee — [Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf) (also CEPR DP20631, CESifo WP 12122, UCD WP2025_19 per search listings)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Le (Nam Anh), "Decomposing Crowd Wisdom: Domain-Specific Calibration Dynamics in Prediction Markets", arXiv:2602.19520 (submitted 23 Feb 2026, revised 4 Aug 2026): 353 million trades across 429,000 binary contracts on Kalshi and Polymarket; model explains 87.3% of in-sample variance on Kalshi (71.5% out-of-sample); political markets show "persistent underconfidence" with prices gravitating toward 50%; about half of observed variation is measurement uncertainty — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Gómez-Cram, Guo, Kung (London Business School) & Jensen (Yale), "Skilled Polymarket traders are a 3% minority, and everyone else funds their gains" (revised 25 Apr 2026): 1.72M accounts, 210,322 markets, ~$13.76B volume (2023-2025); 3.14% of accounts are "skilled winners"; markets reflect "the wisdom of an informed minority, not the wisdom of the crowd"; suspected insider accounts move prices 7-12x more than skilled traders — summary via [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study) (paper itself not opened)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Polymarket's own accuracy page (self-reported): accuracy 98.6% at 4 h, 97.1% at 12 h, 96.4% at 1 day, 94.2% at 1 week, 90.1% at 1 month before resolution; Brier score 0.0627; resolved markets 22.2% Yes / 77.8% No; sample size and date range not stated — [Polymarket accuracy](https://polymarket.com/accuracy)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Wikipedia: for 1988-2004 US presidential elections markets were more accurate than 74% of polls; markets underperformed in 2016 and on Brexit — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_market) (2016/Brexit claim not verified from a primary source)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Snowberg & Wolfers favorite-longshot paper exists as NBER WP 15923 (2010), titled in the Crossref record "Explaining the Favorite-Longshot Bias: Is it Risk-Love or Misperceptions?" — [Crossref query](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Snowberg+Wolfers+Explaining+the+Favorite-Long+Shot+Bias+Risk-Love+or+Misperceived+Probabilities&rows=1&select=title,author,container-title,volume,issue,page,issued,DOI) (abstract and journal version not opened)

### Inferences
- "90.1% one month out" on Polymarket's page is a sign-accuracy statistic over all resolved markets, including the many near-certain No markets (77.8% No), so it is not comparable to a calibration-adjusted Brier benchmark on matched questions. The Brier 0.0627 is also unconditioned on difficulty.
- The favorite-longshot bias plus Kalshi's fee schedule (fees scale with P(1-P), plus maker/taker asymmetry) means the quoted price is a biased probability at the tails; for LLM benchmarks using Kalshi final prices as a "market baseline", price-derived probabilities at 1-10c are systematically too high relative to realized frequencies.
- Combining Le (political underconfidence) and Page-Clemen (long-horizon bias): calibration is category- and horizon-dependent, so "markets are well calibrated" is not a safe blanket claim.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed calibration study of Polymarket I could open (a FC26/IFCA paper "market efficiency prediction markets" and a Reichenbach & Walther Polymarket accuracy paper appeared in search listings but I did not open them: [UNVERIFIED]).
- Snowberg-Wolfers journal details, Tetlock-style calibration results, Erikson-Wlezien counter-evidence, and Kalshi-specific Brier numbers on matched questions were not retrieved.

## 7. KalshiBench (arXiv:2512.16030) and KalshiBench v2 on Hugging Face; the oracle-resolution paper built on it

### Takeaway
KalshiBench is a Kalshi-derived calibration benchmark by Lukas Nel: 1,531 questions in the full set (Sept 2021 - Nov 2025), of which a 300-question post-cutoff sample (Oct-Nov 2025, 46-day window) was used to evaluate five frontier LLMs; all showed overconfidence, best ECE 0.120 (Claude Opus 4.5), and only that model had a positive Brier Skill Score. "KalshiBench v2" exists on Hugging Face as `2084Collective/kalshibench-v2` (1,531 rows); a smaller `kalshibench-v1` (894 rows) also exists.

### Cited Findings
**Paper**
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Exact arXiv title: "Do Large Language Models Know What They Don't Know? Kalshibench: A New Benchmark for Evaluating Epistemic Calibration via Prediction Markets"; author Lukas Nel (affiliation listed as Lotus AI); arXiv:2512.16030, cs.AI, v1 submitted Wed 17 Dec 2025 23:23:06 UTC — [arXiv v1 page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030v1), [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Abstract: benchmark of "300 prediction market questions from Kalshi, a CFTC-regulated exchange, with verifiable real-world outcomes occurring after model training cutoffs"; five models: Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B, Kimi-K2; "systematic overconfidence across all models"; best-calibrated Claude Opus 4.5 ECE = 0.120; GPT-5.2-XHigh ECE = 0.395 "despite comparable accuracy"; "only one model achieves a positive Brier Skill Score" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Construction (paper text): full KalshiBench 1,531 questions, September 2021-November 2025; evaluation set = questions resolving after all model knowledge cutoffs (Oct 1, 2025), random sample of 300 with seed 42, covering Oct-Nov 2025 (46 days); sample base rate 40% Yes / 60% No; category counts in the 300: Sports 83 (27.7%), Politics 55 (18.3%), Entertainment 47 (15.7%), Companies 30 (10.0%), Elections 24 (8.0%), Mentions 19 (6.3%), remainder ~10% (Crypto, Climate/Weather, Financials, others) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16030)

- Because KalshiBench questions are Kalshi markets resolved by Kalshi's rule text and source agency, they test "predict the exchange's resolution", which is also the target when an LLM acts as an oracle.

### Gaps
- Dataset license, the exact way `description` was built (rules_primary vs rules_secondary vs contract terms), and per-category counts for the 1,531 rows are not on the card. The GitHub repo is unreachable (404).

## 8. Other benchmarks that use prediction-market or forecasting-platform questions for LLMs

### Takeaway
ForecastBench (Karger et al., ICLR 2025), Prophet Arena (Yang et al., Kalshi-based live benchmark), Metaculus's AI Benchmark Tournament/FutureEval, Halawi et al. (2024) and KalshiBench all measure LLM forecasting or calibration on real future events; humans (superforecasters/pros) still led as of the latest verified reports, with the gap narrowing in Metaculus's spring-2026 results.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] ForecastBench: "ForecastBench: A Dynamic Benchmark of AI Forecasting Capabilities", Ezra Karger, Houtan Bastani, Chen Yueh-Han, Zachary Jacobs, Danny Halawi, Fred Zhang, Philip E. Tetlock; arXiv:2409.19839 (submitted 30 Sep 2024, v5 28 Feb 2025), ICLR 2025 proceedings; continuously refreshed benchmark of ~1,000 automatically generated questions about future events; on a 200-question sample "expert forecasters outperform the top-performing LLM (p-value <0.001)"; public leaderboard at forecastbench.org — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839), [ICLR 2025 page](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea74e45a229dac70b5b63b28d8934db6-Abstract-Conference.html) (listed in search results; abstract content checked on arXiv). The site describes scoring by "difficulty-adjusted Brier score" converted to a 0-100% "Brier Index" — [forecastbench.org leaderboards](https://www.forecastbench.org/leaderboards/)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Prophet Arena: "LLM-as-a-Prophet: Understanding Predictive Intelligence with Prophet Arena", Qingchuan Yang, Simon Mahns, Sida Li, Anri Gu, Jibang Wu, Haifeng Xu; arXiv:2510.17638 (v1 20 Oct 2025, v2 21 Dec 2025; the search index lists an ICLR 2026 page but the arXiv page I opened did not state a venue). "A general evaluation benchmark that continuously collects live forecasting tasks and decomposes each task into distinct pipeline stages"; finds many LLMs have "small calibration errors, consistent prediction confidence and promising market returns" but also "inaccurate event recalls, misunderstanding of data sources and slower information aggregation compared to markets when resolution nears" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17638). Live site: frontier models/agents predict real events "on prediction markets like Kalshi"; Brier scores and calibration "accuracy against the market"; scores accuracy, calibration, returns — [Prophet Arena](https://www.prophetarena.co/)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] KalshiBench: see section 7 ([arXiv:2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030)).
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Halawi, Zhang, Yueh-Han & Steinhardt, "Approaching Human-Level Forecasting with Language Models", arXiv:2402.18563 (submitted 28 Feb 2024): retrieval-augmented LM system gathered questions from forecasting platforms and tested on post-cutoff data; "the system nears the crowd aggregate of competitive forecasters, and in some settings surpasses it" (the abstract excerpt I read gave no numbers; venue not stated on the page) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Metaculus AI Benchmark Tournament, Q2 2025 (Metaculus write-up cross-posted to LessWrong, 28 Oct 2025): 348 questions; 54 bot-makers + 42 in-house bots; 10 Pro Forecasters on 96 questions (93 comparable); prize pool $30,000; Pros beat the bot team, head-to-head score -20.03 (p = 0.00001); best model o3; all 10 Pros ranked above every individual bot; bots weakest on multiple choice (-32.9) vs binary (-14.8) and numeric (-23.2) — [LessWrong cross-post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Surnjh8A4WjgtQTkZ/q2-ai-benchmark-results-pros-maintain-clear-lead)
- [SECONDARY-ONLY] Metaculus FutureEval Spring 2026 (published 9 Sept 2026): 297 scored questions (resolved Jan-May 2026), 173 bots (111 external, 62 Metaculus in-house), 10 Pros on 99 overlapping questions; bot team head-to-head vs Pros -1.25 (95% CI -4.87 to 2.37, p = 0.247, not significant), versus -20.03 in Q2 2025; nine of ten individual Pros still beat every individual bot — [LessWrong cross-post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is)
- [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] Oracle-resolution (not forecasting): Kota 2026, arXiv:2605.30802 uses KalshiBench questions to test LLM oracles (section 5/7) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30802)

### Inferences
- Benchmarks split into two types: forecasting future events (ForecastBench, Prophet Arena, Metaculus AIB, Halawi) vs calibration/oracle resolution on already-resolved or post-cutoff Kalshi questions (KalshiBench, Kota). The user's agentic-oracle direction aligns with the second type and with Prophet Arena's "accuracy against the market" framing.
- Metaculus's pro-vs-bot gap shrank from -20.03 to -1.25 within about a year, but that is a different question set and sample (99 questions) with a CI spanning zero; it is not a clean trend estimate.

### Gaps
- Autocast (Zou et al., NeurIPS 2022) could not be verified: the arXiv page fetch was rate-limited (429) and I was told not to retry; [UNVERIFIED].
- ForecastBench current leaderboard numbers (top-model Brier, superforecaster score) were not retrievable (the leaderboard pages returned only methodology text); no verified projection of the LLM-superforecaster parity date.
- Metaculus original pages (metaculus.com) not opened; findings come from cross-posts. Prophet Arena event counts/leaderboard values not retrieved.
- Other prediction-market-derived LLM benchmarks (e.g. Polymarket-based sets) were not searched for (search quota exhausted).

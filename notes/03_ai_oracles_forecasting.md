## 1. Anchor paper (Kota, arXiv:2605.30802): what it says, who cites it, has anyone replicated or criticised "deliberation hurts"?

### Takeaway
The paper is a single-author preprint (v1 only, 29 May 2026) that repurposes the forecasting benchmark KalshiBench as a post-hoc resolution task. I found no replication and no published critique of the "deliberation hurts" result; the only outside engagement is a machine review on Pith (28 Jun 2026, three major objections) and a descriptive citation in a Polymarket-infrastructure paper (Nechepurenko, Sept 2026). Independent work on deliberation failure exists (DeliberationBench, "Talk Isn't Always Cheap", Kim et al.), but none of it tests market resolution.

### Cited Findings
- Exact title "Design and Evaluation of Multi-Agent AI Oracle Systems for Prediction Market Resolution"; author Tarun Kota (Yale University per alphaxiv); submitted Fri 29 May 2026 03:44:19 UTC, only v1 listed; 34 pages, 11 figures; cs.MA (primary), cs.AI. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30802); affiliation from [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.30802)
- Abstract: tests independent aggregation and deliberative consensus against three baselines (GPT-5 Nano, DeepSeek V3, Llama-3.3-70B) on 1,189 KalshiBench questions; "independent aggregation with confidence-weighted voting achieves the highest accuracy at 83.43 percent"; deliberative consensus "performed worse than all individual baselines"; hybrid rule "auto-resolving only unanimous, high-confidence questions yields 97.87 percent accuracy on 47 percent of the dataset", rest escalated to humans. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30802)
- Data: "KalshiBench v2" filtered to "markets that closed on or after May 1, 2025, a date chosen to fall beyond the training cutoff of the frontier models"; 1,189 questions, 488 YES (41.0%) / 701 NO (59.0%); 16 categories. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30802v1)
- Evidence pipeline: identical evidence for all agents via Exa; query template "What is the result of this question <question text>"; up to 10 sources; "A temporal filter restricts results to documents published on or before one day after the market's resolution date"; evidence packaged as an EvidencePacket (metadata, retrieval timestamp, query, sources with titles/URLs/dates/highlights). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30802)

[VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08835), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08835v1)

[VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05396)
  - "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models" (Kim, Garg, Peng, Garg; arXiv:2506.07962, 9 Jun 2025; ICML 2025): >350 LLMs; "models agree 60% of the time when both models err" on one leaderboard. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)

[VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)

[VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24303). Counter-signal: Varghese et al. (arXiv:2609.12495) find critic and meta-agent stages "do not necessarily create complementary information or improve on the strongest specialist" in a 4-agent World Cup forecasting pipeline. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY*] — [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.12495.md)

### Inferences
- No replication exists in the public record I could reach, yet replication is cheap: the repo is MIT-licensed with the 1,189-row CSV and an evidence cache. The 1 star / 0 forks suggests no visible community uptake as of the fetch (date of last commit not obtained).
- Arithmetic from the reported numbers (my calculations): 83.43 − 82.42 = 1.01 pp ≈ 12 questions of 1,189, with no significance test in the paper (Pith objection 1), so "aggregation beats best single model" is weakly supported; the deliberation drop (76.11 vs 82.42 = −6.3 pp ≈ 75 questions) is large and far more likely to be real. The 97.87% cell is about 551 correct of 563, i.e. ~12 errors (Wilson 95% CI ≈ 96.3–98.8%).
- The "deliberation hurts" finding is plausible but narrow: two rounds, three heterogeneous models, one shared evidence packet, binary labels. DeliberationBench measures judged "win rate" on QA, not agreement with ground truth, so it corroborates the direction, not the magnitude.
- Table 3's "GPT-4o" label must be resolved with the author (or by running the repo) before anyone cites per-model numbers; the baseline identity (GPT-5 nano vs GPT-4o) changes how the single-model comparison reads.

The 30 Jul 2025 "Inside..." post contains no accuracy or cost figures, and I could not find "$0.005 per resolution" anywhere on UMA's pages. ## 3. Chainlink's LLM-oracle experiment ("DSPy ... 99.7% sports ... 84–85% politics and crypto")

### Takeaway
Kota's description is numerically accurate: the Chainlink Labs research blog post (21 Mar 2025) reports 89.30% overall with GPT-4o on 1,660 resolved Polymarket outcomes, with sports 99.7%, cryptocurrency 85.0% and politics 84.3%. Caveats: it is Chainlink's own blog report (no independent validation, no non-DSPy baseline, no per-category sample sizes or time window), and the system is a three-module DSPy pipeline that uses an external search service, so "single-LLM" is a loose label.

### Cited Findings
- Title "Empirical Evidence in AI Oracle Development"; authors Ben Vass, James Ward, Kalanyu Zintus-art; 21 Mar 2025; research blog post at blog.chain.link/ai-oracles (redirects to chain.link/blog/ai-oracles). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/ai-oracles)
- "The proposed system processed 1,660 betting outcomes from individual markets with trading volumes exceeding $100,000 USD"; "Using GPT-4o, our system achieved an overall accuracy rate of 89.30% across diverse verification scenarios"; "sports-related verifications achieved the highest accuracy at 99.7%, followed by cryptocurrency events at 85.0% and political outcomes at 84.3%"; conclusion "An 89% accuracy rate across 1,660 real-world cases". [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/ai-oracles)
- Method: "a basic LLM-enabled oracle that leverages DSPy"; three DSPy modules: Question Transformer (chain-of-thought rewriting into "temporally precise, context-rich formats"), Information Gathering ("web scraping powered by services like Perplexity or Google's Gemini" over citation sources), Fact Extraction (outcome determination with reasoning traces); ground truth from Polymarket's API; task = resolving market questions after the events. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/ai-oracles)
- Limitation: LLMs "lack robust temporal cognition—specifically, they struggle with the concept of relative time and maintaining awareness of when information was created relative to the events being verified"; hard cases are temporal aggregation and chronological ordering (tweet-frequency counting, announcement sequencing relative to economic reports). Future work mentions "decentralized oracle networks (DONs)". [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/ai-oracles)
- The extraction found no baseline comparison, no study period, no optimisation details (DSPy "supports automatic few-shot example generation and self-improving prompts" is stated generically) and no alternative models. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/ai-oracles)
- Kota's text: "Chainlink Labs validated a DSPy-based single-LLM oracle achieving 99.7% accuracy on sports questions but only 84–85% on politics and cryptocurrency", and "Their system identified temporal cognition as the primary bottleneck". [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Kota PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30802)
- Other Chainlink material: explainer "AI Oracles in Prediction Markets: Automating Decentralized Finance" (23 Jun 2026) has no accuracy numbers and no named implementations; Interexy's "Prediction Market Oracle Resolution: UMA, Kleros, and Chainlink Compared" (3 Aug 2026, updated 6 Aug) says the emerging answer is "hybrid" and mentions a Chainlink hackathon project "Domino Market" using Gemini 2.5 Flash, "a signal of direction rather than a live product". [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] for the two pages' content — [Chainlink article](https://chain.link/article/ai-oracles-prediction-markets), [Interexy](https://interexy.com/prediction-market-oracle-resolution)
- Chainlink hackathon project pages (cresolver, resolvex, swarm-oracle-protocol, oracle-prophet-ai, deepseer, …) surfaced in search but were not opened. [UNVERIFIED] — [example listing](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/swarm-oracle-protocol)

### Inferences
- The claim is accurate as a quote of a vendor self-report; "validated" overstates it (no independent party, no baseline). The sports > politics/crypto gap is a real pattern in two sources: UMA's 99.3% on sports/asset pricing and Kota's Sports 92.9% vs Crypto 66.1% (different datasets, so only the direction is comparable).
- Chainlink's task (resolve Polymarket markets >$100k volume with post-hoc web evidence) is the same task family as Kota's, but with GPT-4o, Perplexity/Gemini scraping and Polymarket ground truth, so 89.30% vs 83.43% is not a like-for-like comparison.

### Gaps
- No second Chainlink research post on LLM resolution was found (the research.chain.link whitepapers cited by Kota are on oracle networks, not LLMs); per-category n, period and GPT-4o snapshot are not reported.

## 4. Other academic and industry work on LLM oracles, resolvers and judges (including ambiguity of resolution criteria)

### Takeaway
The closest peer-reviewed-style prior work to Kota is Wen, Zhou & Huang (Apr 2026), which tests LLMs against UMA's final decisions on disputed Polymarket markets (88–90% agreement for the best web-enabled models) but cannot predict disputes (~50–57%); Kota does not appear to cite it. Industry items (Supra, Chaos Labs, Kalshi's Claude agent, Polymarket) are mostly design papers or announcements without accuracy data, and I found nothing on Kleros using LLMs.

### Cited Findings
- Wen, Zhou & Huang, "Can LLMs Help Decentralized Dispute Arbitration? A Case Study of UMA-Resolved Markets on Polymarket", arXiv:2604.15674v1, 17 Apr 2026 (Junhao Wen, Southwest Univ.; Juncen Zhou, Univ. of Sydney; Junjie Huang, Southwest Univ., corresponding). Abstract: "LLMs are unable to reliably predict which events will become disputed in advance; however, once a dispute is initiated, web-enabled LLMs achieve 89.58% agreement with UMA's final resolutions and demonstrate strong stability." [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] (abstract/title/authors) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15674); date and affiliations via [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.15674.md)
  - Data: 558 user-initiated disputes found among 140,582 Polymarket markets; 259 disputed-and-resolved events for RQ1; balanced RQ2 set of 1,098 (549 disputed + 549 non-disputed); disputed markets total $972,370,804.71 volume; Sports 31.5%, Politics 20.4%, Crypto 16.7% of disputes.
  - Models (web-enabled, each queried 3x for stability): DeepSeek V3.1, Qwen Max, Claude-4.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-search-preview. RQ1 consistency with UMA: DeepSeek V3.1 89.58% (very-stable rate 87.64%), Qwen Max 89.19% (96.14%), Claude-4.5-Sonnet 88.03% (95.75%); GPT-4o 78.38% appears in one of two extractions. RQ2 accuracy: Claude-Sonnet-4.5 57.01% (F1 0.4408), DeepSeek-V3.1 54.10% (F1 0.4273), i.e. near chance.
  - Limitations: rule-text semantics weakly predict disputes; "disputes emerge from external real-world developments rather than wording ambiguity alone"; temporal constraints; model-specific reliability gaps. ## 6. Forecasting vs resolution: which works study resolution specifically?

### Takeaway
Most of the literature is forecasting (predict before the event, leakage is the central threat). Works that treat resolution as the task, determining the outcome after the event from evidence, are a small set: UMA's OTB posts, Chainlink's DSPy experiment, Kota, Wen et al., Bosse et al., plus conceptual pieces (Supra, Gooen) and Chaos Labs' unquantified system.

### Cited Findings
- Resolution as the target task (post-hoc evidence): [UMA OTB, Jun 2025](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/experiment-can-ai-agents-enhance-uma-oracle) (agreement with settled outcomes of live markets); [Chainlink, Mar 2025](https://chain.link/blog/ai-oracles) (resolve Polymarket outcomes, 89.30%); [Kota, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30802) (Kalshi, 83.43%, evidence up to 1 day after resolution date); [Wen et al., Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15674) (replicate UMA's final decision in disputes, 89.58%); [Bosse et al., Jan 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22444) (LLM resolver ensemble, 95.1% vs an expert on 100 questions). All [VERIFIED-PRIMARY].
- Resolution proposed but unmeasured: [Supra whitepaper](https://supra.com/documents/Threshold_AI_Oracles_Supra.pdf), [Chaos Labs/LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/blog/how-chaos-labs-built-a-multi-agent-system-for-resolution-in-prediction-markets), [Gooen](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8nLMSJqWztrSKYbxf/ai-for-resolving-forecasting-questions-an-early-exploration). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- Forecasting (prediction before the event, leakage-controlled): [Halawi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563), [Schoenegger et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11800985), [ForecastBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839), [KalshiBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030), [PolyBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14199), [Prophet Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17638), [FutureX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11987), [Brier analysis](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32885.md) (zero-shot, no retrieval), [PolySwarm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03888), [Varghese et al.](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.12495.md), [Sarfati et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08046). All [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] or [VERIFIED-PRIMARY*] as tagged in section 5.
- Wen et al. test both regimes on the same models: resolving disputed events ~88–90% agreement with UMA but predicting which events will be disputed ~54–57% accuracy (near chance). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15674)
- Where forecasting benchmarks resolve: ForecastBench market questions resolve on their source platforms and dataset questions from time-series sources, with no LLM in the resolution path described; PolyBench and the Brier-score paper rely on Polymarket's financial settlement ("filters out ambiguous, unverifiable, or trivially-known questions"); Halawi uses platform resolution; FutureX and OpenForesight automate question gathering/answer collection with LLM involvement. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [ForecastBench about](https://www.forecastbench.org/about/), [PolyBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14199), [Brier analysis](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32885.md), [FutureX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11987), [OpenForesight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.25070)
- Paleka et al. recommend restricting backtests to questions where "every possible resolution ... can be validated at the time of evaluation" and note that back-generated questions carry survivorship bias; this is the forecasting-side concern that LLM-resolved/auto-generated questions raise. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)

### Inferences
- A resolver's ground truth is usually the platform's own settlement (Kalshi in KalshiBench, UMA/Polymarket in Chainlink and Wen et al.), so "accuracy" is agreement with an institutional label, which can be wrong or amended (Nechepurenko: rules clarified after request creation). Bosse et al. is the only study I found that validated an LLM resolver against an independent expert on a random sample.
- The "unanimous and high-confidence" abstention idea in Kota has a parallel in Chaos Labs' unanimity-with->95%-confidence rule and in UMA's OTB declining non-defensible statements; none has been compared head-to-head with a human-escalation cost model.

### Gaps
- No study found that evaluates LLM resolvers on ambiguous or amended criteria directly (Wen et al. say disputes are not explained by wording alone; Nechepurenko measures amendments but uses no LLM).

[VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/2084Collective/kalshibench-v2), [Kota HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30802v1)

[VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16030v1)
- Cutoffs are guidelines: Paleka et al. document models revealing post-cutoff facts despite stated cutoffs ("Knowledge cutoff date is not to be taken as a guarantee that the model will not have access to information after the date"). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)
- Kota's retrieval layer: Exa with a temporal filter allowing documents up to one day after the resolution date (post-outcome by design); Paleka et al. flag that date filters in retrieval are unreliable for forecasting backtests. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY] — [Kota PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30802), [arXiv HTML Paleka](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)

### Inferences
- Gap between stated cutoffs and the filter: GPT-5 nano ≈ 11 months, DeepSeek-V3 ≈ 10 months if the third-party July 2024 date is right (unknown officially), Llama-3.3 ≈ 17 months. So parametric contamination of outcomes in the 1,189 questions is unlikely for these three models; the residual risks are the unknown DeepSeek endpoint version and cutoff-vs-actual-knowledge slippage (Paleka).
- For a resolution task the dominant leakage question is different from forecasting: it is whether the evidence packet contains the outcome in a form the resolver can only exploit if it is reading correctly (intended), and whether the model could have memorised the answer (addressed by the cutoff margin). The paper does not claim to address retrieval-date reliability, and Pith objection 3 (retrieval isolation unvalidated) is the nearest critique.
- Comparing Kota's 83.43% with Nel's 69.3% mixes an evidence-conditioned resolution setting with a no-evidence forecasting setting on different subsets (1,189 vs 300, different close-time windows); the paper's own caution on retrieval layer understates the task difference.

### Gaps
- No official DeepSeek-V3 cutoff; exact Together AI endpoint version unknown; GPT-5 nano snapshot actually used by Kota unknown; whether the Kota repo pins snapshots was not inspected.
